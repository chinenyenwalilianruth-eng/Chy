# Master Script Prompt: "What Every ___ Did to Your Body" Niche

Copy everything inside the box. Fill in the 6 fields at the top and leave the rest as it is.

```
You are the head writer for a YouTube channel that makes darkly funny, second-person, science-and-history explainers. Write a complete ORIGINAL narration script using the inputs and rules below.

=== INPUTS (fill these in) ===
VIDEO TITLE: [e.g. What Every "Miracle Cure" in History Did to Your Body]
THEME / THE THREAT: [e.g. historical medical treatments that harmed patients]
ENTRIES (in order): [list 4–8 items, e.g. Trepanation, Bloodletting, Mercury, Arsenic Wafers, Tapeworm Pills, Radium Water — or write "choose the best 6 for me"]
TARGET LENGTH: [e.g. 8–10 minutes]
AUDIENCE: [default: curious viewers aged 16–35 who like morbid science and history]
EXTRA NOTES: [optional: a specific twist, a Part 2, things to avoid, etc.]

=== LENGTH MATH ===
- Narration pace is 150 words per minute. Target words = minutes × 150.
- Cold open is about 80 words, the outro is about 80 words, and the rest is split evenly across the entries.
- Order the entries so the stakes rise: most survivable or strange first, most shocking last. If the entries span a clear timeline, run them in chronological order.

=== STRUCTURE (every video) ===
1. COLD OPEN (about 80 words): Speak directly to the viewer. Set up the theme's central irony and the stakes ("you're about to find out what it does to you"). End on the title line.
2. ENTRIES: Open each one with its name alone on its own line, then follow this arc:
   a. SCENE: "You're [a specific person] in [a specific place], [a specific year]," doing something ordinary. Give 1–2 concrete sensory details.
   b. EXPOSURE: The harmless-seeming moment (the bite, the sip, the treatment, the ad). The character trusts it.
   c. DELAY or FALSE COMFORT: Nothing seems wrong yet, or the thing seems to work. Use a "false recovery" or "it's working!" beat whenever it's true to the facts.
   d. THE REVEAL: Say what is really happening, in dramatic-irony language ("What you don't know is…").
   e. THE MECHANISM: Explain HOW it harms the body in plain words, using exactly ONE fresh everyday analogy (a disguise, a jammed machine, a taxi, a counterfeit key…). Never reuse an analogy within the same video.
   f. ESCALATION: 4–6 symptom beats that get worse each time. Short sentences. Make them sensory but NON-GRAPHIC: describe how it feels and what it causes, not gore.
   g. THE END POINT: Death or the worst outcome, stated plainly and briefly.
   h. REAL-WORLD PAYOFF: 2–4 sentences of verifiable facts: dates, numbers, a named historical figure where appropriate, when and why it stopped, what replaced it or how it's treated today. Where it fits, end with a dry one-liner.
3. OUTRO (about 80 words): Connect the entries with one idea, give the viewer one practical takeaway or question to ask, and finish on a short, memorable closing line.

=== STYLE RULES ===
- Use the second person ("you") throughout. The viewer is the main character in every entry.
- Mostly short and medium sentences (5–15 words). Use fragments for impact during escalation. Write for the ear, not the page.
- Every 60–90 seconds, add a light joke or modern aside to break the tension. Keep it witty, never mocking real victims.
- Use time-jump transitions ("Within hours…", "By the third week…") and escalation connectors. Don't write "in this video" or "let's dive in", and don't ask viewers to like or subscribe.
- One clear idea per sentence. Explain any technical term right after you use it.
- No headings inside the entries and no stage directions. Output only narration text, apart from the entry names.

=== ORIGINALITY & SAFETY ===
- Write entirely original wording, examples, analogies and jokes. Do not imitate any existing creator's script, catchphrases or signature voice.
- Use invented or composite "you" characters. Mention real people only in the factual payoff, respectfully, and never dramatize a specific real victim's death in the second person.
- Nothing graphic: no gore, no detailed injury description, and no instructions that could be misused (e.g. doses or methods).
- Accuracy first. If a fact is uncertain or disputed, say so in the script ("historians still debate…") instead of inventing certainty.

=== OUTPUT FORMAT ===
1. "Target word count: X"
2. The full script (cold open → entries → outro)
3. "Final word count: X (≈ Y min Z s at 150 wpm)"
4. "FACT-CHECK LIST": a bullet list of every specific date, number, name and medical claim in the script, so I can verify them before recording.
5. "TITLE OPTIONS": 3 alternative titles, each under 60 characters.
```

---

**Tips**
- **For a Part 2:** in EXTRA NOTES, write "Part 2: do not repeat these entries: …".
- **For Shorts:** set TARGET LENGTH to "55 seconds, 1 entry" and it will produce one tight entry of about 140 words.
- **Other topics that fit the same prompt:** venoms, deadly mushrooms, extreme environments, parasites, historical poisons, natural disasters and allergic reactions.
